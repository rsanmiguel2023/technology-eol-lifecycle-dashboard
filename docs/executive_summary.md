# Executive Summary

## Technology Lifecycle Governance Dashboard

Maple Financial Bank maintains **23,770 managed technology assets** across end-user computing, infrastructure, network, cloud, and enterprise application environments. The current lifecycle assessment identifies **4,682 assets already past vendor support**, representing **19.7%** of the managed estate.

The analysis also identifies **8,337 assets expiring within 12 months**, **3,367 assets expiring within 24 months**, and **5,402 assets expiring within 36 months**. This creates a near-term refresh pipeline of **17,106 assets** that requires active funding, sequencing, and governance.

Cybersecurity exposure is concentrated in **1,391 unsupported or near-EOL assets with critical/high vulnerability exposure**. The largest concentration is in **Laptop** assets, but the exposure also includes network, server, desktop, VDI, and storage assets, making this a cross-platform risk rather than an endpoint-only issue.

Software lifecycle analysis identifies **91,618 unsupported software installations**. The largest compliance drivers include Adobe Acrobat Pro 2020, Google Chrome 124, Microsoft Teams Classic, Windows 10 Enterprise 22H2, and OneDrive 24.x.

Refresh planning estimates **$70.8M** in required investment across FY2026–FY2029. The largest forecast year is **FY2027**, requiring approximately **$25.6M**.

The highest-risk business unit is **Retail Banking**, with an executive risk index of **110.0** and a **Critical** risk band. This business unit has the highest combined exposure across unsupported assets, vulnerability concentration, downtime, and replacement cost.

## Executive Decision Message

Technology lifecycle management should be treated as a governance and risk reduction program, not only an infrastructure refresh activity. The recommended priority is to remediate unsupported vulnerable assets first, then fund the multi-year refresh roadmap, and then institutionalize lifecycle governance controls.
