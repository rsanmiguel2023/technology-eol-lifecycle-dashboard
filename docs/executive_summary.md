# Technology Lifecycle Executive Summary

## Management Message
Maple Financial Bank's synthetic technology estate includes **23,940 managed assets** across end-user computing, servers, network infrastructure, storage, and cloud-connected services. The current lifecycle position requires executive attention because **4,634 assets (19.4%) are already past EOL**, **3,661 unsupported assets have critical vulnerabilities**, and the near-term refresh requirement is **CAD $56.8M**.

The dashboard should be read as a technology risk management view, not as an inventory report. It connects asset lifecycle, cyber exposure, compliance status, refresh funding, and operational disruption into one management narrative.

## Executive Questions
1. Which assets are already past EOL?
2. Which business units have the highest EOL exposure?
3. Which unsupported assets also have critical vulnerabilities?
4. How much budget is needed for refresh planning?
5. Which software versions create the largest compliance risk?
6. How does EOL status relate to incidents and downtime?

## Executive Summary
The estate contains **4,634 past-EOL assets** and another **7,354 assets due within 12 months**. The highest combined exposure is concentrated in **Retail Banking**, followed by **Technology**. Cyber risk is material because **3,661 past-EOL assets** carry at least one critical vulnerability across endpoints, servers, firewalls, routers, switches, wireless infrastructure, and storage.

Refresh planning indicates **CAD $56.8M** in lifecycle replacement need across assets past EOL or due within 24 months. Current earmarked refresh allocation is modeled at **CAD $18.8M**, leaving a planning gap of approximately **CAD $38.0M**. Operationally, past-EOL assets account for **79,128 downtime hours** and show higher downtime per asset than supported assets.

## Recommended Executive Decision
Approve a governed 24-month lifecycle remediation roadmap with three immediate priorities: isolate or replace unsupported assets with critical vulnerabilities, fund the near-term refresh gap, and assign business-owned remediation plans to the highest-risk units.
